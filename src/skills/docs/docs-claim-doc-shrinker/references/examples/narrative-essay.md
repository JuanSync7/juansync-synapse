# A Summer Afternoon in the Old House

The light came in low through the kitchen window, the kind of light that makes you think about people who used to live somewhere. The fridge hummed. Outside, the cicadas were doing what cicadas do, which is to say nothing in particular for hours on end.

I had come back to clear out the house, but I sat at the table for a long time without clearing anything. There was a coffee ring on the wood that I remembered from twenty years ago, or thought I did; memory is funny that way. You can be certain a thing happened, and then you put your hand on the wood and the certainty thins.

My grandmother used to say that a house keeps the shape of the people who lived in it. I had never known what she meant until I sat there listening to the fridge and watching dust move through the light. The shape of someone is, it turns out, mostly silence and a particular angle of afternoon sun.

I made tea. I did not clear anything that day. I sat with the silence until the silence felt less like absence and more like company, and then I locked up and drove back to the city. The boxes would be there tomorrow. They had waited this long. They could wait another night.
